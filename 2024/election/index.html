Candidate statements for the
2024 IEEE SCAM Steering Committee Election

Banani Roy

Statement: I have been an active contributor to the SCAM community, publishing regularly at the conference and enjoying the opportunities it provides for collaboration and knowledge sharing. My leadership in conference organization, including roles as Program Co-Chair of SCAM 2022, General Chair of IWSC 2023, currently the Tool Demonstration Co-Chair of ICPC 2025, further demonstrates my commitment to fostering the software engineering research community. My background in producing high-quality, impactful research, my dedication to mentoring the next generation of researchers focusing on source code analysis and manipulation, and my enthusiasm for participating in and contributing to SCAM align with the steering committee's goals of advancing the study and practice of source code analysis and manipulation. I am eager to bring my expertise, leadership experience, and collaborative spirit to the SCAM steering committee to help shape its strategic vision and support its continued growth as a platform for innovation in software engineering research.

Bio: I am an Assistant Professor and Director of the Interactive Software Engineering and Analytics Lab at the University of Saskatchewan, with extensive experience in research, mentorship, and community engagement in the field of software engineering. My research focuses on human-centric software development and renovation, as well as software analytics, contributing to building reliable, scalable, and cost-effective tools for software development and maintenance. As a co-applicant on the NSERC CREATE SOAR program and a researcher in two CFREF projects, I have led collaborations involving academia and industry, mentoring over 20 graduate and undergraduate students, many of whom have achieved notable accolades, including Best Supervisor, Best Paper and Best Poster awards, as well as prestigious scholarships.

Coen De Roover

Statement and Bio: I’m a professor at the Software Languages Lab of the Vrije Universiteit Brussel in Belgium. My research interests are the design of program analyses and their application within software quality. They are positioned at the confluence of PL and SE, just like the SCAM conference. I’ve therefore published several papers in its proceedings, and its mugs occupy more than a shelf of my kitchen cupboard. I’ve served as program chair of the 2017 edition in Shanghai, and as general chair of the 2024 edition in Flagstaff. In between, I served as a member of the steering committee where I lead its CORE ranking re-evaluation effort. I would like to serve again with the aim of ensuring SCAM maintains its discussion-oriented format, while gradually improving its ranking and attracting more members to our community from e.g., PL.

Tushar Sharma

Statement: I gladly accept the nomination for the SCAM steering committee. SCAM is one of my favorite conferences, and I sincerely appreciate the opportunity to contribute to its continued growth and impact. If selected, my primary focus will be expanding the conference's reach and influence. By actively engaging with academic networks and creating outreach programs, I aim to increase submission volumes and attract more students and emerging researchers to participate. I envision seeing SCAM ascend the ladder of conference rankings and reputation, and I will put in all my efforts to realize this vision.

Bio: Tushar Sharma is a tenure-track assistant professor at Dalhousie University, Canada. He leads the Software Maintenance and Analytics Research Team (SMART) lab in the Faculty of Computer Science. Topics related to software quality, sustainable artificial intelligence, and machine learning for software engineering (ML4SE) define his career interests. He earned a PhD from the Athens University of Economics and Business, Athens, Greece, specializing in software engineering in 2019. He has ten years of industry experience, mainly with Siemens Research, USA and India. He co-authored Refactoring for Software Design Smells: Managing Technical Debt and two Oracle Java certification books. He founded Designite, offering code quality assessment tools that many practitioners and academic researchers belonging to at least 190 universities use worldwide. He is an IEEE Senior Member.

Masud Rahman

Statement: Since 2014, I have been attending the SCAM conferences. Thanks to the SCAM community for their nomination; it has been an honour. Source code is still the king! However, given the gradual shift from logic-driven software code to data-driven AI models (e.g., LLM) and their emerging capabilities, we must rethink source code and the SCAM community’s role moving forward. As an SC member, I would contribute to and lead such discussions, develop innovative methods to extend the community (e.g., social media campaigns) and strategize to improve SCAM’s outreach and ranking.

Bio: Dr. Masud Rahman is a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the Faculty of Computer Science at Dalhousie University, with a Ph.D. in Computer Science/Software Engineering from the University of Saskatchewan. His research focuses on understanding and tackling software maintenance challenges, emphasizing automation in software debugging, code reviews, and code search, and targeting general-purpose, AI, and simulation modelling software systems. His recent works investigate the challenges of detecting and reproducing errors in AI systems and attempt to design novel, effective software solutions for them. Dr. Rahman leads the RAISE Lab at Dalhousie University and has supervised 13 students, including five Master's and four PhD students. Masud has published his works at the top Software Engineering venues, including ICSE, FSE, ASE, TOSEM, and EMSE. He has received several awards, including the Governor General's Gold Medal, the TCSE Distinguished Paper Award, and the Distinguished Reviewer Award. His work has secured over $475K (as PI) and over $4M (as Co-PI or Co-applicant) from sources like NSERC, Mitacs, CAAF, and Dalhousie. Dr. Rahman collaborated with academics (e.g., Polytechnique Montreal) and industry partners (e.g., Mozilla Firefox, MetaBob Inc., Vendasta Technology) for his research.
